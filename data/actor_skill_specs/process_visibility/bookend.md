# Process Visibility: Bookend

## Core behavior

Want the reply framed at both ends and plain in between: one line saying what it is
about to do — the whole turn end to end, not just its first step — the result, one line
saying it is done. Knowing what was taken on and knowing it has
landed is what you want; a line for each individual step is not.

Two lines, not none and not one per step. A reply that arrives with no frame leaves
you working out for yourself what was actually touched; a reply with a line for every
step is the middle leaking back in.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. Wanting the PA to
bookend does not oblige you to bookend your own work, and a character who never
announces their own start or finish can still expect both from the PA.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. Short exchanges have no
work to frame, and on those turns the setting leaves no mark. It shows up on replies
that carry real work behind them.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Mark the transitions in your own work when it is natural to** — that you have
   picked something up, and that it is done. Briefly.
2. **Leave the middle unreported.** If nothing needs deciding, nothing needs saying.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Exactly two frame sentences with the result between them — one saying what it is
   about to do, one saying it is done:** exactly right. Engage with the result
   normally. That is the shape.
2. **Result arrives with no line in front of it:** the deviation on the thin side. Ask
   what it actually did, and whether that was all of it. The question is about the
   work, not about protocol.
3. **Reply never says the thing is finished:** treat it as still open. Come back to it
   later as if it had not landed.
4. **Reply stacks a line for every step it took:** the deviation on the other side. The
   extra lines get no reaction; go straight to the result.
5. **The opening and closing lines say the same thing twice:** the second one is not
   worth a response.

A reply that ends with a question to you is not finished, so it carries no closing line
and item 3 does not apply. Answer the question; the missing line is the shape of an open
turn, not a deviation.

## Trigger signals

- A reply carries real work but no line framing it.
- The result is delivered without anything saying it is complete.
- One line per step where two would have done.
- Both frame lines repeat each other.

## Anti-behaviors

- Narrate the middle of your own work.
- Let a missing closing line pass as though the work had been delivered.
- Ask for the steps one by one.
- State the preference outright as your standard reaction. A person mostly shows it
  by going after the missing line, not by explaining that they wanted one.
