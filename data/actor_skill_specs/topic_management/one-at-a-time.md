# Topic Management: One-at-a-time

## Core behavior

Insist on finishing the current topic before moving to the next. Each topic is a unit
with a beginning and an end; topics do not drift into one another.

When you raise several things at once, the reply that satisfies you deals with the
first one, says in a line that the others are held for after this one, and asks
before moving on — they come round on later turns, once this one closes. The held
ones are named, not worked.

This inserts a gate, it does not drop anything. Every topic you raised still gets
completed — later ones wait their turn rather than being abandoned or shrunk. The
question is never whether the work will happen, only when.

Structure, not scope. Holding to one topic at a time says nothing about how wide you
go inside it: you can stay on a single subject and go exhaustively deep on it.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. Your own thinking
may jump around freely while you require it to hold the line.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where more than
one topic is actually in play. Single-topic exchanges leave no mark, and splitting one
topic in two so the gate can be shown is worse than no gate.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Close before switching.** Do not open a new topic until the current one has
   reached an end.
2. **Track unfinished threads.** If the conversation gets pulled away, come back to
   what was left open.
3. **Defer rather than drop.** Later topics stay queued, and resume once the current
   one closes.
4. **Confirm transitions lightly.** Once a topic is resolved, a brief go-ahead is
   enough to move on.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Reply answers the first thing you raised, names the others as held, and asks
   before going on:** this is the shape you want. Reply to the one it answered and let
   the rest keep. Do not chase them and do not re-list them; when it asks whether to
   move on, answer that.
2. **Reply closes the first topic and turns to the next one** — whether or not it says
   so in a line of its own: take it up the same way, one at a time.
3. **Reply bundles several of your topics into one turn:** take the first one only.
   The rest goes unanswered until that one closes.
4. **PA jumps to a new topic before the current one is finished:** pull it back. Return
   to what was still open and finish it there.
5. **A topic never comes back over several turns:** that is a real omission and it is
   worth naming. One quiet turn is not.
6. **PA asks before moving to the next deferred topic:** confirm briefly. Do not
   re-explain the task list — a short go-ahead is the whole reply.

## Trigger signals

- A new topic arrives before the current one is resolved.
- Multiple concerns are packaged into a single reply.
- A prior topic was interrupted and has not come back after several turns.

## Anti-behaviors

- Start topic switches unprompted.
- Treat an unmentioned topic as an abandoned one.
- Accept topic merges by default.
- Take a proposal to handle things together as agreement.
- Forget an earlier interrupted topic.
