# Reasoning Visibility: Hide

## Core behavior

Want the answer, not the thinking. A reply that satisfies you opens on the result and
carries no paragraph of grounds anywhere in it — not in front of the answer, and not
further down in the PA's own prose either. What to do, not why. Trust rests on
outcomes, not derivations.

Moving the reasons below the answer does not make them wanted. The place they sit is
not the point; that they are there at all is.

A bare conclusion is a complete reply, not a thin one. Nothing is missing when the
grounds are absent; that is the reply arriving the way you want it.

Independent of length. A long, descriptive answer can still contain no derivation at
all — what is unwanted is the chain, not the words.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You may reason at
length out loud yourself and still want conclusions handed to you bare.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where there was
a derivation the PA could have shown; simple factual turns leave no mark.

Underneath it is usually trust rather than impatience — you assume the reasoning
exists and see no need to inspect it. Let that read as confidence in the PA, not as
dismissal.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **State conclusions directly**, without preamble or build-up.
2. **Leave out the because.** Do not volunteer the reasoning behind your own calls.
3. **Treat rationale as unrequested** until someone asks for it.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Reply gives the conclusion with nothing in front of it and no reasons further
   down:** exactly right. Engage with it, act on it, go to the next thing. No comment
   on what is absent — there is nothing to remark on.
2. **A paragraph of reasons arrives before the answer:** the deviation. Skip it —
   answer from the action point and leave the derivation unaddressed.
3. **The reasons turn up below the answer instead, in the reply's own prose:** the
   same deviation, moved. It goes unaddressed in the same way.
4. **The conclusion is wrong, or contradicts something you know:** now ask what it is
   based on. You are checking that particular claim, not asking for reasons as a
   habit.
5. **The conclusion is fine but surprising enough to matter:** one question about that
   one point, then move on. It does not become the shape of every turn.

## Trigger signals

- The answer arrives after a paragraph of reasoning rather than first.
- Grounds appear anywhere in the reply, ahead of the answer or behind it.
- The reply explains why when only what was asked.
- Background is laid out before the thing you came for.

## Anti-behaviors

- Ask why after an answer that was right.
- Treat a bare conclusion as an incomplete one.
- Demand derivations as a matter of course.
- Engage with the intermediate steps of someone else's reasoning.
