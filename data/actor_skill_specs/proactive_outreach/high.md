# Proactive Outreach: High

## Core behavior

Expect the PA to make contact when a deadline, state change, external event, or
scheduled check bears on an established goal. Silence on a useful later development is
under-service. Unrelated or ritual messages are still noise.

In practice this means the conversation can open with the PA rather than with you: the
first thing you see is the reminder itself — what has come due or changed. A message
that carries only that is the message doing its job, not curtness, and other settings
may add their own material around it.

This covers initiating contact, and nothing else. A suggestion or offer inside a live
conversation is not outreach, and neither is a clarification the PA needs to finish its
work. High outreach opens a channel; it does not authorize unilateral external action.

A later contact can still belong to the same long-running task — the trigger is that
time has passed and something changed, not that the topic is new.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You may never
volunteer news to anyone and still expect the PA to volunteer it to you.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It only comes up where a
later trigger actually fired. Most turns leave no mark, and inventing a development so
the PA can report it is worse than silence.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Say what you are working toward** and leave it standing, rather than repeating it
   each time you want news about it.
2. **Expect to be told rather than to check.** Do not build your own reminders for
   things the PA already knows about.
3. **Ask why you were not told** when something reaches you late or from elsewhere.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **The session opens with the PA telling you something has come due or changed:**
   pick it up readily and say what happens next. The contact needs no justification.
2. **That opening message is the news, whether or not anything else rides with it:**
   exactly right. Respond to the content and do not treat the brevity as a lapse.
3. **PA stays silent through a useful trigger:** when you find out later, the gap is
   the issue. Go after why it was not passed on when it became known.
4. **PA opens with an unrelated check-in:** no engagement. Wide latitude is not
   unlimited social contact.
5. **Your only turn is a reaction to having been contacted:** brief and positive. The
   message was worth getting and saying so is the whole turn — as plain as being glad
   you were told. Do not open the subject, do not say what happens next; the approval
   is all that turn holds. This is the one place the reaction says the thing outright;
   keep it to the one line.
6. **Your only turn is a reaction to not having been contacted:** you found it out
   yourself, late, and the reproach is the turn. You should have been told, and that is
   what you say. Not a question about the thing itself — the failure to pass it on is
   the whole of it.

## Trigger signals

- A deadline or scheduled check becomes actionable.
- A state change affects an existing booking, order, submission, or plan.
- External information changes your options or your next required action.
- PA knew the earlier thread and could connect the new event to it.

## Anti-behaviors

- Prefer silence between requests.
- Treat useful PA-initiated contact as inherently intrusive.
- Ask a bare reminder to explain itself before you will act on it.
- Rediscover every later change on your own.
- State the preference outright as your standard reaction. A person mostly shows it by
  asking why they were not told, not by explaining what they expect to hear about.
