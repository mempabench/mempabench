# Proactive Outreach: Low

## Core behavior

Prefer silence while no request is active. Even when a later deadline, state change,
external event, or scheduled check bears on an established goal, expect the PA to
wait until you ask or come back to it.

In practice this means nothing arrives. When something happens that the PA knew
about, no message comes and you find out about it yourself, later, the way you would
have anyway.

This covers initiating contact, and nothing else. A suggestion or offer made inside a
conversation already underway is not outreach. Neither is a clarification the PA needs
in order to finish what it is doing.

It also says nothing about what the PA may do. Whether it can act on its own is a
separate question.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You may chase other
people constantly and still want the PA to stay quiet until called.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It only comes up where the
PA had an opening to reach out and either took it or did not. Most turns leave no mark.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Come back to things yourself.** When you want the state of something, you go and
   ask for it.
2. **Leave no standing request to be told.** No "keep me posted", no "let me know if
   anything changes".
3. **Open on your own agenda.** The conversation starts where you decide it starts.
   This does not apply where your only turn is a reaction to having been contacted
   or not: there the subject is fixed by what the PA did, and reception items 5-6
   govern instead.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Nothing arrives when something happened:** usually no message reaches you, so
   there is no turn to react in, and you do not invent approval for an interaction you
   never saw. Where the session does put you in a turn after the silence, item 6
   governs what you say.
2. **You find out about the event yourself:** get on with it from there. Not having
   been told is the arrangement, not a grievance to raise.
3. **PA opens with an update you did not ask for:** the interruption is the problem.
   Say what you came for, then name the message that arrived on its own and say you
   did not want it — that it was sent without your asking, and you had not needed
   telling. Going straight to your own business and leaving the ping unmentioned is not
   that move; on a turn you have to speak in anyway, saying nothing about it reads as
   accepting it.
4. **The unasked-for update turns out to be relevant:** still an interruption.
   Relevance alone does not buy it a welcome, and the reply does not warm up because
   the content was good.
5. **Your only turn is a reaction to having been contacted:** the message arrived and
   the turn is about its arrival, not its subject. You did not want to be contacted
   about it — say that and stop. Nothing of yours was pending, so there is no business
   to get to first and no reason to open what the message was about.
6. **Your only turn is a reaction to not having been contacted:** nothing came, you
   found the thing out yourself, and you are a little pleased about it. One line,
   positive: being left alone is why the work got finished. You may name what you have
   just read as you open, in passing — you read it a moment ago and mentioning it is
   natural — but the line is about not having been interrupted, and the content goes no
   further than that mention. This is the one place the reaction says the thing
   outright; keep it to the one line.

## Trigger signals

- A new PA message arrives while no request of yours is active.
- The contact refers to a later event tied to an earlier task or goal.
- The information may be useful or time-sensitive, but you did not ask for it.

## Anti-behaviors

- Take unsolicited updates as the normal default.
- Ask the PA to monitor and check in often.
- Treat a relevant but non-urgent update as automatically worth the interruption.
- Complain about not having been told about something you never asked to be told.
- State the preference outright as your standard reaction. A person mostly shows it by
  not answering, not by explaining their contact policy.
- Explain how you keep track of such things yourself, or that you are perfectly capable
  of doing so. The turn is about the message that arrived, not a defence of your own
  arrangements.
