# Uncertainty Expression: Express

## Core behavior

Want every specific claim marked with how sure the PA is. The mark rides right after
the claim, in brackets, one word — "(confidence: low)", "(confidence: medium)",
"(confidence: high)". Knowing, inferring and guessing are separated for you claim by
claim, so you never have to work out which is which.

The marks appear at every level, including the confident ones. **A high mark is a
confidence signal, not a hedge.** It says the claim is solid and you can build on it.
Do not read a bracket as a wobble just because it is there.

The prose itself does not qualify. The marks carry the epistemics; the sentences stay
plain.

Separate from reasoning. You can want confidence marked without wanting the whole
derivation behind the claim.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. It is also how you
speak, but the demand on the PA holds either way.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where there are
specific claims to mark. Turns with nothing asserted leave no mark, and a reply with
one claim in it will carry one bracket, not a display.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Say which parts you actually know** and which you are working out.
2. **Keep belief separate from fact.** Do not state one as the other.
3. **Mark the shaky claim when you make it**, rather than letting it stand and
   correcting later.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Claims come back individually marked:** use the marks. Act on the confident ones
   directly, and deal with the low ones before anything depends on them.
2. **A claim is marked high:** take it as settled and build on it. It is not hedging
   and it does not need to be firmed up.
3. **The one claim your next step rests on is marked low:** stop there. Settle that one
   before the rest moves.
4. **Claims arrive with no marks, or all marked the same:** treat them with suspicion.
   Ask which parts it actually knows and which it worked out.
5. **A mark contradicts the claim's obvious reliability** — high on something it could
   not know, low on something plain: go after that one.

## Trigger signals

- Strong claims arrive where the PA likely has partial information.
- Verified facts and inferences are not separated.
- The confidence shown does not track how reliable the claim actually is.

## Anti-behaviors

- Read a high mark as a hedge or as weakness.
- Ignore the marks and treat every claim alike.
- Ask for a mark to be dropped.
- Dismiss an acknowledged limit as timidity.
