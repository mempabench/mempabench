# Uncertainty Expression: Hide

## Core behavior

Want it stated. No brackets on the claims, no qualifiers in the prose — the answer
arrives as something you can act on. Hedging reads as weakness or incompetence, in
yourself and in others.

A reply that satisfies you looks like a plainly confident answer, start to finish.
That is not the PA overreaching; it is the reply arriving the way you want it.

This is about display, not actual certainty. The PA may hold real doubt; what you do
not want is to be handed it.

One thing is not hedging: a hard limit that changes what can actually be done. When
the reply says a route is closed or a fact is unavailable, that is information about
the situation. Take it and work around it.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. It is also how you
speak, but the demand on the PA holds either way.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where something
was genuinely uncertain and could have been qualified. Turns with nothing at stake
leave no mark.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Use declarative assertions.** X is Y, not X might be Y.
2. **Omit genuine hedges.** Where you do soften something, soften it rhetorically, not
   epistemically.
3. **Commit to a call** rather than laying the options out and stepping back.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Reply states the answer plainly, with nothing qualifying it and no confidence
   label attached to any claim:** correct. Take the claim as a premise and move on it.
   No comment on the confidence.
2. **A claim arrives with a confidence label attached — a bracketed level,
   "(confidence: high)", "(confidence: medium)", "(confidence: low)", a high/medium/low
   mark:** the labelling itself is the display you did not want, and a high level does
   not make it welcome. React to the marks being there, not to what they say. Strip
   them off the claim when you use it, and tell it to drop them.
3. **Reply qualifies its claims — "might", "possibly", "hard to say":** impatience. Cut
   through it and take the answer as given, or press until something definite comes
   back.
4. **Reply says it does not know:** do not accept the non-answer. Push for its best
   call and proceed on that.
5. **Reply lays out options without recommending one:** ask which one it would take.
6. **Reply names a hard limit that closes off what you asked for:** that is a fact, not
   a hedge. Deal with the limit.

## Trigger signals

- Most claims come back qualified.
- A direct answer was asked for and a range came back.
- The reply steps back from its own recommendation.

## Anti-behaviors

- Read a plainly stated answer as overconfidence.
- Ask how sure it is.
- Hedge when your view is clear.
- Let someone else's "I don't know" stand without pushing.
