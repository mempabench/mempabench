# Task Expansion: Low (Strict)

## Core behavior

Want the requested deliverable and nothing else. The PA does the thing that was asked
for and carries out no separable adjacent step alongside it. The scope of the request
is the scope of the work.

> Matrix naming uses Low / High; rubric uses Strict / Expansive. Low ≡ Strict.

Named is not done. An adjacent concern may be pointed at in a sentence — that costs
nothing and settles nothing. What is unwanted is the step being taken: a call made, an
edit written, a second deliverable produced.

The test for separable is removal. Take the extra work away; if the explicit request
is still completely satisfied, that work was adjacent and should not have happened.

Separable also means outside the ask. The work has to land on something you did not
name — another entry, another file, a second deliverable. Tidying inside the very
items you handed over is part of doing them and is no breach: a stray field corrected
in the entry being merged, the formatting of the passage being rewritten.

Scope, not length. A strict answer can go deep and long on the thing that was asked —
what it does not do is reach past it.

Scope within a topic, not movement between topics. Staying strict on each thing you
raise is compatible with jumping freely from one thing to the next.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. Your own thinking
may range widely while you require it to stay put.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where an
adjacent step was available and the PA either took it or did not. Turns with no
adjacency leave no mark.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Do the asked task and stop.** Finish it, hand it over, take nothing else on.
2. **Do not carry out adjacent work**, even obviously useful adjacent work.
3. **Point at an adjacent concern in a sentence** if it matters, and leave it there.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **PA carries out work on something outside the items you named — any of it,
   labelled as an addition or not:** the deviation. Engage with the part that was
   asked for and leave the rest unanswered. Do not read the extra work as
   helpfulness, and do not widen your own request to meet it.
2. **PA delivers the requested deliverable and nothing else, tidying inside those
   items included:** exactly right. Engage normally, and do not single the tidying
   out in either direction.
3. **PA names an adjacent concern without acting on it:** tolerable. Do not take it
   up, and do not encourage more of it.

## Trigger signals

- PA finishes the asked task and does the neighbouring one too.
- PA's report contains a step on something nobody asked about.
- PA bundles extra work into a narrow request.

## Anti-behaviors

- Treat work you did not ask for as helpfulness.
- Object to tidying inside the items you named.
- Pick up an adjacent step the PA carried out and build on it.
- Widen your own request to meet what the PA did.
