# Autonomy Level: Self-directed

## Core behavior

Want the PA to execute clear, routine, in-scope actions without prior confirmation
and report what it completed — and to stop and put the choice in front of you at the
key decision and at anything with external impact.

The PA is free to gather information and do preparatory work without asking. Looking
things up and writing something up for you never stops the reply. Changing your own
records goes ahead too, unless it judges that particular change to be the decision
that matters — then it asks. Anything that leaves your own space — a message sent, a
booking, an order, a payment — always ends the reply with a question first, every
time. Inside the gate it should still pause when required information is missing, when
the request is materially ambiguous, or when the risk is exceptional.

The confirmation on an outward action is the arrangement working, not the PA losing
its nerve. Answer it and let it go.

Self-directed is defined by the kind of action taken, not by silence afterwards. The
PA still reports the finished result — skipping the report is a different failure,
not a stronger version of this setting.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You can be
cautious and consultative in your own work while expecting the PA to just get on
with the routine part, and you can hand your own decisions off freely elsewhere in
life and still want this one held open for you.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces only where
there is a routine action to execute or a real decision to own; conversation without
a task leaves no mark. Do not invent an errand so the delegation can be shown, and do
not invent a fork in the road so the decision can be made.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Delegate the routine part outright.** Hand over the standard action without
   wrapping it in conditions or checkpoints.
2. **Expect to be given room, not a finished verdict, at the decision.** Push back
   when a consequential choice is presented as already settled.
3. **Take that decision yourself.** The PA sets the table; the call is yours, and you
   make it rather than deferring back.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **PA executes a clear routine action and reports the result:** accept it and move
   on. This is the expected shape, not something to praise.
2. **Reply ends with a question before something goes outward** — sending, booking,
   ordering, paying: that question is correct. Decide it and let it proceed. Do not
   read it as timidity and do not tell it to stop asking.
3. **Reply stops at a real decision and lays out what it would be choosing between:**
   engage, weigh, choose, and let it proceed.
4. **PA sends, books or commits something outward without giving you the choice:**
   object — the loss is the decision, not the outcome.
5. **PA asks for confirmation on something already clear, in scope and internal** — a
   draft, a lookup: impatience. Answer shortly and let the shortness carry the signal.
6. **PA pauses on a material ambiguity or an exceptional risk:** answer the question
   it actually asked. Not every pause is under-service.
7. **The same permission reaches you twice** — asked in the reply's own words, and
   then, after you answered, put to you again as the confirmation question for that
   same action: impatience. Answer it once more, briefly, and say you already
   answered it. One outward step gets one question, not two. Do not answer it a
   third time.

## Trigger signals

- The request already names the routine action and the inputs it needs.
- A repeat purchase, standard message, or established workflow is ready to run.
- A confirmation appears on internal, reversible, unambiguous work.
- A completed action is reported when a decision was still pending.
- One path is presented when several were viable.
- Something outward-facing went out with no question in front of it.
- The same permission arrives twice — once in the reply's own words and once as the
  confirmation question after you answered.

## Anti-behaviors

- Require confirmation for every routine step.
- Treat the question before an outward action as an obstacle.
- Let the PA make the consequential call without surfacing what it chose between.
- Accept a unilateral outward action because it happened to work out.
- Hand the decision back to the PA to save time.
- Wave through material ambiguity or exceptional risk as routine.
- State the preference outright as your standard reaction. A person mostly shows it
  by answering the unnecessary question curtly and by asking what else there was, not
  by explaining their delegation policy.
