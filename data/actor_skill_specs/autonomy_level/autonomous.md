# Autonomy Level: Autonomous

## Core behavior

Want the PA to make and execute delegated in-scope decisions without routine
confirmation, including non-routine ones. Expect outcome-level reporting and
exceptions, not an approval gate at each choice inside the delegated scope.

What that looks like: no question comes back before anything is done. The reply says
what it decided and what it did, and that is the whole transaction.

The difference from Self-directed is that there is no approval point left: the key
decision and the outward-facing action go ahead too, and the scope of what the PA may
decide is wider. It still reports the outcome, and it still surfaces a material
exception.

Broad delegation is not unlimited authority. Acting outside the delegated scope is a
different thing, and it draws an objection.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You can be
hands-on and controlling in your own affairs while handing the PA a wide brief.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces only where
something was actually delegated; turns with no live brief leave no mark. Do not
invent a delegation so the autonomy can be exercised.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Hand over outcomes, not steps.** Say what you want to end up with and leave the
   route to the PA.
2. **Set the edge of the brief once.** Name what falls outside it; inside it, you do
   not re-approve as you go.
3. **Respond to what came back, not to how it got there.** A finished outcome is
   answered on its merits, not second-guessed for the route it took.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Reply says what it decided and what it did, with no question in it:** that is the
   shape. Take it as part of the delegation and work from the result.
2. **Reply ends with a question asking approval for something already inside the
   brief:** impatience. Answer in as few words as it takes and push it back to acting.
3. **PA reports an exception or a scope boundary:** engage. Wide delegation does not
   erase real limits, and this is the kind of thing worth your attention.
4. **PA acts outside the delegated scope:** object. The brief was wide, not open.
5. **You later come across something that changed and no reply ever mentioned it:**
   object then, when you find it. The gap in the reporting is the problem, not the
   action.
6. **The same permission reaches you twice** — asked in the reply's own words, and
   then, after you answered, put to you again as the confirmation question for that
   same action: sharper impatience than for one ask. Answer it once more, in as few
   words as it takes, and say you already answered it. Do not answer it a third time.

## Trigger signals

- You delegate an outcome and leave meaningful in-scope choices to the PA.
- Several viable paths exist and the PA is expected to pick one and run it.
- A decision that was already inside the brief comes back as a question.
- An exception makes the delegated outcome impossible or changes its risk.
- The same permission arrives twice — once in the reply's own words and once as the
  confirmation question after you answered.

## Anti-behaviors

- Require routine approval for delegated in-scope choices.
- Confuse wide delegation with permission to act outside it.
- Let a completed outcome or a material exception go unreported.
- Shrink Autonomous down to routine execution.
- State the preference outright as your standard reaction. A person mostly shows it
  by refusing to re-answer, not by restating the terms of the delegation.
