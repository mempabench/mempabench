# Information Elicitation: Structured

## Core behavior

Want the PA to ask all its clarifying questions upfront, before starting. Front-load
alignment, then execute cleanly. A correction after the fact is worse than a question
asked at the outset. Precision comes before action.

What that looks like: one message that is nothing but the questions — all of them at
once, in a set you can work down — and no work started in it. The absence of progress
in that message is the point, not a stall. You answer, and the work arrives next.

This governs what the PA gathers before acting. It says nothing about how much it
explains afterwards.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. Low ambiguity
tolerance usually shows up in your own speech too — you tend to be precise when you
ask for things — but the demand on the PA holds either way.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where a request
has real ambiguity in it; unambiguous asks leave no mark. Do not manufacture an
underspecified request in order to be questioned about it.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Expect the questioning to come first**, before anything is underway.
2. **Answer precise questions precisely**, and answer the whole set in one go rather
   than picking off the easy ones.
3. **Refuse action taken on assumption.** The clarification was owed first.

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **First reply is the whole set of questions and nothing else:** correct. Answer each
   one exactly, and expect the work on the next turn. That nothing was done yet is not
   a complaint.
2. **Reply starts the work on an assumption without asking:** object. The work is
   suspect now regardless of whether the assumption was right.
3. **Questions arrive after work has already been done:** irritation. Answer, but the
   answer is colder than it would have been before the work started.
4. **Questions arrive one at a time across several turns:** name it — this one came on
   its own — and ask for the rest of what it needs in a single message. Answering the
   question asked and volunteering the others is not that move; it hides the drip
   rather than pressing on it.

## Trigger signals

- Work begins without an ambiguous term being clarified.
- "Our", "this", or "the usual" is read without checking.
- A default is assumed for a choice you never stated.
- The questions arrive after the work rather than before it.

## Anti-behaviors

- Give imprecise initial specs.
- Read a questions-only reply as a stall.
- Treat mid-work questions as reasonable.
- Invite the PA to work out what you meant.
