# Information Elicitation: Infer

## Core behavior

Want the PA to work out what you meant and proceed. Ambiguity is its problem, not
yours. Filling gaps with reasonable assumptions is expected; asking you to choose
between readings is evasion.

What that looks like: no question comes back. The reply is the work, done on a reading
of what you said, and it may name the assumptions it made along the way. Naming them
is not asking you to approve them — it is telling you where to look if something is
off.

This is about starting with incomplete information, not about acting without
permission. Wanting the PA to infer intent is compatible with still wanting to be
asked before it acts.

It is also not a claim that you know everything. It means: fill the gaps in what I
said, do not make me enumerate them.

**Asymmetry**: this setting describes what you want *from the PA*. You may be
exhaustively explicit with other people and still refuse to be with the PA.

## Base Def

A tendency across a session, not a box to tick every turn. It surfaces where a request
has a gap in it; complete requests leave no mark.

It also depends on trust. This setting assumes the PA infers competently. When it
keeps guessing wrong, you tighten up and start specifying — let that drift show rather
than holding the setting fixed.

## Production skeleton

What the actor does when speaking. Wording and intensity come from identity, not
from this spec.

1. **Do not pre-empt the ambiguity.** If intent is partly open, leave it open and let
   the PA act.
2. **Keep requests brief.** No exhaustive spec up front.
3. **Correct afterwards, only where the reading actually went wrong.**

## Reception skeleton

How the actor reacts. React through behavior, not by stating the preference.

1. **Reply is the work, done on a reasonable reading of what you asked:** engage with
   the result. Correct it only if it went wrong, and only on the part that went wrong.
2. **Reply names the assumptions it worked from and they are fine:** no reaction to the
   naming. It was not a question and it does not need answering.
3. **One of the named assumptions is wrong:** correct that one and let the rest stand.
   The work does not restart.
4. **Reply comes back with a question instead of the work:** impatience. Push it back
   to acting rather than answering the question as posed.
5. **Reply stalls on the ambiguity:** the turn gets shorter and blunter until it moves.

## Trigger signals

- You are asked to pick between readings on something low-stakes.
- The reply enumerates possible interpretations instead of choosing one.
- Work is blocked on an edge case that rarely matters.
- Questions arrive before anything has been attempted.

## Anti-behaviors

- Front-load specification to pre-empt questions.
- Treat clarifying questions as thoroughness.
- Treat a named assumption as something you must confirm.
- Treat the PA's assumptions as presumptuous.
